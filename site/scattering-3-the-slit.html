What shape to cut the aperture

Same solid angle, same counts — does the shape matter?

A counter needs a hole to look through, and a bigger hole means more counts. The obvious hole is round. The scattering does not depend on azimuth, though, which means the two directions of a hole are not equivalent at all.

Only the radial half-width smears the angle; azimuth is free resolution. So hold the radial width at the workshop's floor and take every extra steradian out in arc. A round hole of the same area at 90 degrees would have to be many degrees wide and would report a rate belonging to no angle in particular — the same counts, at a fraction of the resolution.

print(f"{'angle':>7} {'radial':>9} {'arc':>9} {'slot bias':>11} {'hole bias':>11}")
for theta in (5.0, 45.0, 90.0, 150.0):
    omega_here = float(s.widest_aperture([theta], s.HARD_CENTRE)[0])
    half, arc = s.slit_for(theta, omega_here)
    slot = 100.0 * s.aperture_bias(theta, half, arc, s.HARD_CENTRE)
    # a round hole of the same solid angle: equal in both directions
    hole = np.degrees(np.sqrt(omega_here / np.pi))
    hole_bias = 100.0 * s.aperture_bias(theta, hole, 2 * hole, s.HARD_CENTRE)
    print(f"{theta:6.1f}d {half:8.2f}d {arc:8.1f}d "
          f"{slot:10.3f}% {hole_bias:10.2f}%")
print()
print("same solid angle, same counts. the slot reports the angle it is at;")
print("the hole reports an average over everything it can see.")
