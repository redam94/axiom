Confounders, colliders and mediators

“Control for everything you have” is the single most expensive piece of folk statistics in circulation. A variable's position decides whether adjusting for it removes bias, creates bias, or answers a different question — and the three positions are indistinguishable from the correlations alone.

The three shapes

Same three variables, three different worlds

In all three, X is what you might change, Y is what you care about, and Z is the variable you are deciding whether to put in the model. The arrows are different, and that is the whole difference.

Staff numbers and patient outcomes: sicker wards get more staff. Leave severity out and staffing looks harmful.

Admission to a study requires being either very ill or very rich. Among those admitted, wealth predicts health — in the world, it did not.

Filters reduce illness by improving water quality. Adjust for water quality and the filter's effect vanishes — correctly, and uselessly.

Why you cannot tell them apart from the data

All three shapes above produce correlations among X, Y and Z. The confounder and the mediator can produce identical joint distributions. Which one you are in is a claim about what causes what — knowledge from outside the dataset — which is exactly what a causal graph is for writing down.

The collider deserves its own section

Adjusting can create the bias it was meant to remove

The confounder story is well known. The collider one is not, and it is worse, because the fix people reach for is the thing that causes it.

Suppose two entirely unrelated things — say, a candidate's technical skill and their interviewing skill — both help you get hired. Among people who were hired, the two become negatively correlated: someone who got in with weak interviewing must have been technically strong, and vice versa. Look only at employees and you will discover that technical skill hurts interview performance. It does not. You conditioned on the thing they both cause.

This is why “I put in every variable I had” is not a conservative choice. Every variable you had includes the ones caused by the treatment and the ones that decided who is in the sample at all. Each of those turns a clean comparison into a contaminated one, silently.

Selection is a collider you did not choose

If being in the dataset at all depends on both the treatment and the outcome — survey responders, surviving customers, patients who came back — you have conditioned on a collider before writing a line of code. The education example works one of these end to end.

The rule that actually works

Never adjust for anything caused by the treatment. Beyond that, do not pick the adjustment set by hand at all — write the graph, and let identification derive a licensed set from it. On the identify page axiom names that set as Z before anything is fitted.

Summary

The table to keep

Confounder X ← Z → Y · a common cause. Leaving it out biases the estimate; adjusting removes the bias. adjust
Collider X → Z ← Y · a common effect. Adjusting creates a correlation that was not there. never
Mediator X → Z → Y · on the path. Adjusting gives the direct effect only — a different, valid quantity that is usually not the one the decision needs. depends
Instrument Z → X → Y, with no other route to Y · a lever on the treatment. Not adjusted for — used, as a separate route when the back door is shut. use
