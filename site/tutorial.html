Tutorial · eight steps

One question, carried all the way

Most library tours show you a subpackage at a time. This one takes a single decision and carries it through every phase it actually has to survive — because the hard part of causal work is not any one of those phases, it is that they have to agree with each other at the end.

A logistics operator runs 40 distribution depots in one region and 500 nationally. Should the standing weekly maintenance schedule go from nothing to 60 hours per depot?

The operator already has a panel: depots, weeks, maintenance hours, an outcome index. The obvious move is to fit it. Step two is where axiom refuses — the depots that were already in good shape got more hours, and "good shape" was never written down, so no adjustment available in that data recovers the effect.

So an experiment gets designed, sized against the boundary the decision turns on rather than the effect anyone hopes for, and run. Then its answer has to be folded back into a model that can be asked the question the trial never measured directly — the trial reads one week, the decision lives in the steady state.

The punchline is in step six. The observational model says fund it. The experiment says do not. Both numbers are on this site, and both were computed when it was built.
