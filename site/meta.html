Pillar 05 · axiom.meta

What does all the evidence say?

One study is an anecdote with a standard error. A programme accumulates dozens, from different teams, on different scales, some of them randomized and some of them model-based reads that everyone quietly trusts less. Pooling that is not averaging. It is deciding what is comparable, how much the studies really disagree, and whether one kind of evidence runs systematically high.

Before any arithmetic

Refusing to pool what is not comparable

Eleven studies report a dimensionless standardized contrast. A twelfth reports the same quantity per acre. Averaging the twelve is a category error, and axiom will not do it silently.

Unsupported — the corpus is refused, with the reason

“study 's12' reports 'standardized_contrast' on a scale (dimension 1, unit_scale 'per_acre'); pooling is over dimensionless quantities and no TransferPlan supplied”

The way through is not a flag that turns the check off. It is to supply a TransferPlan — the same object the calibration layer uses — which states how the per-acre reading maps onto the dimensionless one and carries that claim as a hashed record. Once the plan is attached, the study is admitted with its provenance intact, and the pool runs over 12 studies.

The finding

Model reads run high, and pooling naively buries it

The classical pool has no way to express “these four studies are a different kind of evidence”. axiom’s Bayesian pool carries an explicit bias term for the model-based reads, and first checks whether that term is identified at all — it is only identified because two contributors reported both kinds, which is what pins the two scales together.

The difference is not cosmetic

Pooling everything together gives 0.702. Separating the two kinds of read gives an experiment-scale mean of 0.627. If the number is going to be used to set a prior for the next study, the second is the one you want — and the gap between them is roughly the size of the effect people would be arguing about.

Diagnostics

Which studies are driving the answer

Two views of influence, because “drop it and see” and “how much heterogeneity does it contribute” are different questions and can disagree.

Two ways out

Hand it forward, or publish it safely

As a prior for the next study

The pooled result is not a full stop. It converts into a prior on the amplitude of the next dose–response fit, carrying a handoff ledger line so the eventual model records where its prior came from.

prior lognormal on the predictive, moment-matched — mean 0.627 μ=-0.472
handoff “lognormal prior on predictive of 'standardized_contrast' in family 'fertilizer': moment-matched to mean 0.62708, sd 0.0626557” prior_from_pool

That is the loop closing: the surface that produced each study’s estimate is the same object that now receives the pooled prior.

As a published figure, under a privacy budget

When the corpus spans contributors who will not share raw results, axiom checks a release policy — minimum contributor count, no single contributor dominating — then adds calibrated noise and charges the privacy budget.
