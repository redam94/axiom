Guide · transportability

Why a model does not travel

Every check in the previous guides can pass, and the number can still be wrong where it lands. Identification asks whether an effect is recoverable from these data. Transportability asks the question nobody writes down: recoverable for whom, and does that match who you are about to decide for.

The situation

Nothing went wrong, and the answer is wrong

A household water-filter programme was evaluated in district A. Uptake was recorded per household, illness days were counted over twelve weeks, and identification was settled properly. The estimate is defensible: filters cut reported illness by -1.84 days per period, with a standard error of 0.41.

The decision is in district B. The ministry budgets per person, not per household, and plans over a year, not twelve weeks. District B is also older — a third of district A is under five, against a fifth of district B — and that matters, because filters help small children most.

Nobody is proposing anything dishonest. Somebody is about to put -1.84 into a slide about district B, and three separate things about that number are not true there.

This is the failure that survives every other check

A confounder shows up as a biased estimate you can hunt for. A bad prior shows up in a posterior predictive check. A transfer that was never written down shows up as nothing at all: the number is correct, the interval is honest, the code has no bug, and the answer is to a question nobody asked.

measured district A · per household · 12 weeks -1.84
needed district B · per person · 52 weeks ?

Step one

Name what differs, before arguing about it

A quantity in axiom is an Estimand, and it is the same quantity as another only if all eight of its facets match. Comparing the two here gives the argument its actual shape: three facets differ, and they are not the same kind of problem as each other.

population district A's age mix is not district B's, and the effect depends on age. This one is a claim about mechanism, so a graph can settle it. graph
window twelve weeks caught part of the effect of a filter; a year catches more of it. This one is a claim about dynamics, so the carryover kernel settles it. surface
level a household is not a person, and five people in one house are not five independent observations. This one is a claim about aggregation. design

Written down, the disagreement stops being “is this number applicable” — a question nobody can win — and becomes three specific questions with three different owners. That is most of the value, and it happens before any mathematics.

Step two

A selection diagram: where two worlds differ

Populations always differ somehow. The question is never “are they different” — they are — but whether they differ in a way that changes this effect. That is a claim about mechanisms, so it is written on a graph.

Once the difference is a node, ordinary graph reasoning answers the question. If the S-node can be separated from the outcome by conditioning on some measured set Z, the source study's Z-specific effects can be reweighted to the target's own distribution of Z. That set is called S-admissible, and the reweighting is the transport formula.

What axiom returns for this diagram

P*(illness|do(filters)) = Σ_{age} P(illness|do(filters),age) P*(age)

S-admissible set: age — so district A's age-specific effects, reweighted by district B's age mix. This is why the strata weights on Population are not decoration.

The verdict is not the word “yes”

It is a formula, the set that licenses it, a named assumption — s_admissibility — and the thing that would refute it: a mechanism difference between the populations the diagram omits. An answer you cannot attack is not an answer you can trust.

Step three

Four things a graph can say

The same question, asked against four different beliefs about what differs between the districts. Only one is the easy case, and one of the others is better news than it looks.

identified only the age mix differs. The transport formula applies: reweight the source's effects by the target's own distribution. Route s_admissible_adjustment. age
identified illness itself behaves differently there. Route trivial — and this is the surprise. If the target population can identify the effect from its own data, you do not need the source study at all. The right move is to stop arguing about transfer and go and estimate it there. age, sanitation
downgraded sanitation differs, and nobody measured it. A set that would license the transfer exists — but it contains something nobody recorded, so the formula is not computable from what you have. Downgraded, not refused: the route is named so you know what to go and measure. sanitation
unsupported illness differs and age was never measured. None of the three sufficient conditions holds. Note the word: unsupported, not impossible. sid_recursion

Why the last one does not say “no”

axiom implements three sufficient conditions for transportability, not the complete decision procedure. When none of them fires, a valid transfer may still exist that these three cannot see. Saying “not transportable” would be a claim about the world; saying unsupported with sid_recursion attached is a claim about the evidence, which is the only one the library is entitled to make.

Step four

What the assumptions cost

A graph settles one facet out of the three. Twelve weeks is not fifty-two weeks and a household is not a person, and no causal graph has an opinion about either. Each remaining facet is licensed by a named assumption, and each assumption comes with an operator that says what the correction actually is.

factor on the estimate — the share of the filter's effect that a twelve-week window missed 1.2290
offset nothing here is additive 0.0000
se scale on the standard error alone — the design effect of reading a household measurement per person 1.7678

What the study measured

-1.84 ± 0.41

district A · per household · 12 weeks

What it becomes where the decision is

-2.2615 ± 0.8908

district B · per person · 52 weeks — the point estimate grew, and the standard error grew by a factor of 2.173

The widening is the honest part, and it is the part a spreadsheet never does. Reading a household-level measurement at the person level does not make the effect less certain by accident. It makes it less certain because five people in one house share a water source, and pretending they are five independent observations is exactly the mistake that produces intervals nobody should believe.

Step five

The thing you actually hand over

Not the number. The number, plus every boundary it crossed and what was assumed to get it across. That list is built by construction rather than by discipline, and it refuses to be incomplete.

facet:population population differs: district_A vs district_B
rests on s_admissibility — challenged by overlap; moderator interaction in meta satisfied
facet:window 0.813636 of the carryover mass lands within 12 period(s); scaled to the steady-state total by 1.22905
rests on carryover_contained — challenged by half-life against window length unverified
facet:level cluster read at individual level: point factor 1 (additive effect), SE factor 1.76777
rests on linear_aggregation — challenged by the Jensen gap under a nonlinear response unverified
transport transport verdict identified via s_admissible_adjustment
rests on s_admissibility — challenged by a mechanism difference between the populations the diagram omits satisfied

Completeness

identified

every differing facet accounted for exactly once (population, window, level) — a ledger that skipped a differing facet would fail this check rather than reaching a reader.

Overall status

downgraded

Not identified, and correctly so: 2 of the 4 lines are still unverified. Nothing here has checked the half-life against the window or the Jensen gap against a nonlinear response. The status describes the evidence; it is not a grade.

What to take away

Three habits, in order of how much they save you

01

Write down the question you need before you look at the one you have

Almost all of the value on this page came from the first step, which was free. Naming three differing facets turned an unwinnable argument about whether a number “applies” into three specific questions with three different owners.

02

Make the difference between populations a node, not an adjective

“Different population” is not a finding. S[age] is: it says exactly which mechanism differs, it can be argued with, and once it is on the graph the question of whether that difference matters has a mechanical answer.

03

Let the correction widen the interval

Every honest transfer costs precision. If a number crosses three boundaries and comes out just as certain as it went in, the uncertainty did not vanish — it stopped being recorded. Here the standard error grew 2.173×, and that is the finding.
