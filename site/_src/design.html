Pillar 02 · axiom.design

What should I measure next?

Sample-size calculators answer a narrow question: how many units to detect an effect of a size you nominated. axiom asks the wider one. What do you already believe? What would the experiment actually change your mind about? What is that worth, what does withholding treatment cost, and after both — is this experiment worth running at all?

Step one

Start from what you already believe

A pilot has been run and fitted. Before choosing a size, axiom looks at the posterior for the contrast you care about and asks whether the conventional threshold is even in question.

Over-powering is not free. Every extra unit in the control arm is a unit not receiving a treatment you mostly believe works, and that cost shows up explicitly in the next section rather than being left as a footnote.

Step three

What is the answer worth?

An experiment is only worth running if it can change a decision. Where the value comes from

The curve is steep at the right and flat at the left: buying precision is worth a great deal until the decision stops being in doubt, and almost nothing after. Had the threshold been {{identify.sensitivity.tipping.threshold:.1f}} instead — comfortably below the prior — the whole curve would sit near zero, and the correct recommendation would be to run no experiment and act now.
