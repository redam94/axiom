Map the dose–response

Effects saturate. Doubling a dose rarely doubles a response, and a linear model asked to describe a saturating world will get the optimum wrong in a predictable direction. The surface layer is where axiom keeps the actual shape — and keeps exactly one implementation of it, so the likelihood, the design maths, and the optimizer cannot drift apart.

The experiment

{{surface.design.n}} arms, chosen rather than guessed

Two treatments, each with a Hill kernel. The arms come from a rotatable central composite design over the dose box — a classical response-surface layout that spends its runs where curvature is estimable, instead of spreading them evenly and learning the shape badly everywhere.

The check that earns its keep

A good fit is not the same as an identified one

The curves above track the truth and the posterior predictive checks all pass. That is not sufficient. Several parameters are trading off against each other so strongly that the data cannot separate them — the fit is fine as a prediction and unreliable as an explanation, and those two uses call for different decisions.

Acting on it

Where the optimum is, and what a budget buys

Given a fixed total dose to spend across both treatments, axiom finds the split that maximises the expected outcome — pushing the posterior through the same forward the likelihood used, not a linearised stand-in.

Regret is the number that matters

The fitted allocation puts {{surface.allocation.fitted.a:.1f}} on a where the oracle would put {{surface.allocation.oracle.a:.1f}} — a visible gap in the parameters. The outcome cost of that gap is {{surface.allocation.regret:.4f}} against an achievable {{surface.allocation.oracle_outcome:.2f}}. Near an optimum the surface is flat, so being somewhat wrong about where the peak is costs far less than being wrong about the shape. That is worth knowing before spending more on precision you will not use.

The budget frontier

Re-solving the allocation across budgets gives the frontier and, from its slope, the shadow price — what the next unit of budget is worth. It falls monotonically, which is saturation showing up where a decision-maker can actually use it.

Rule five, in the wild

When the answer does not exist, you get a refusal

Asking for the slope of the response at zero dose looks like a reasonable request. For a Hill curve with a shape parameter below one it is not: the slope at zero is infinite. Here is what axiom does with that.

Unsupported — returned, not raised, and not fudged

{{surface.refusals[0].call}}

→ {{surface.refusals[0].type}}: “{{surface.refusals[0].reason}}”

It names the count and the cause. It does not return inf, it does not silently drop the offending draws, and it does not raise an exception that a caller might catch broadly and lose. The marginal curves on this page are computed on a grid that starts one step above zero — which is a choice the caller makes, having been told why.
