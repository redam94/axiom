Can the data answer it?

Every estimator returns a number. The question that decides whether the number means anything comes first: given how the world is wired, is this effect recoverable from what you measured? axiom answers that from the graph, before any data is touched — and when the answer is no, it says which variable is missing rather than returning a number anyway.

Being told no is the feature

Three outcomes, three different things to do next. Identified means adjust and proceed. Downgraded means the route exists on paper but a variable it needs is missing — so either go and measure it, find an instrument, or bound the damage with a sensitivity analysis. Blocked means no observational route exists in this graph at all, and the only way forward is to intervene: run the experiment.

That last branch is the bridge to the next pillar. When identification fails, the answer is not a cleverer estimator — it is a design.

The rescue

An instrument puts it back in reach

If something shifts the treatment without touching the outcome except through it, the effect becomes recoverable again. axiom finds the instrument in the graph, runs two-stage least squares, and checks the relevance condition rather than assuming it.

Exclusion — the assumption that the instrument affects the outcome only through the treatment — is not testable from data, and axiom does not pretend otherwise. It goes into the verdict as an unverified assumption, which is where a reviewer can find it.

When you cannot fix it

How strong would the confounder have to be?

Sometimes there is no instrument and no way to measure the missing variable. The remaining honest move is to say how bad the problem would have to be to change your mind — and then let a reader judge whether a confounder that strong is plausible in their setting.

Robustness value

{{identify.sensitivity.rv:.1%}}

A confounder would have to explain {{identify.sensitivity.rv:.1%}} of the residual variance in both the treatment and the outcome to wipe the effect out entirely. That is an enormous confounder — which is the point of quoting the number.
