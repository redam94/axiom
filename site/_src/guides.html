The ideas the rest of this site assumes

The tutorials show axiom doing things. These pages explain what the things are, for a reader who has not met them — what makes a question causal, why a confounder is not just another variable, and why a study that was right where it ran can be wrong where it is used.

You do not need Python to read any of them. Where a page shows code, the output beside it is what that code printed when this page was built.

If you are short of time

Three ways through

“I need to make a decision from data I already have.”

Read prediction is not intervention, then confounders, colliders and mediators, then identification comes before estimation. That is enough to know whether your data can answer your question at all — which is usually the finding.

“Somebody handed me a number and I have to use it.”

Go straight to the estimand in eight facets and then why a model does not travel. Most numbers that turn out to be wrong were not wrongly computed. They were correctly computed for a slightly different question.

“I have to defend this in six months.”

Provenance first, then what would have to be true to overturn this. The useful artefact is not the estimate. It is the list of things you assumed and what each one would take to break.
